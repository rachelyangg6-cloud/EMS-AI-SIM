## Dispatch
Twenty-four-year-old female, difficulty breathing, at a community soccer field.

## Presentation
A young woman sits on the bench leaning forward with her hands on her knees. She is wheezing loudly and can say three or four words between breaths. A teammate holds an inhaler the patient says "isn't doing anything."

## Vitals
RR 28, pulse 112, SpO2 91%, BP 132/84.

## Correct actions
1. Ensure the scene is safe and take Standard Precautions.
2. Check her mental status and assess her breathing — work of breathing, lung sounds, words per breath.
3. Sit her upright in a position of comfort.
4. Apply a nonrebreather mask at 15 L/min.
5. Ask about her inhaler use — how many puffs and when — and her history of prior attacks.
6. Assist with her prescribed albuterol inhaler per medical direction.
7. Reassess breathing and SpO2 after the treatment.
8. Transport and call ahead to the receiving hospital.

## Rationale
She is in respiratory distress but still compensating: alert, speaking in short phrases, moving air. Oxygen and a bronchodilator address the problem while the trip begins. Knowing how much of her own inhaler she has already used tells you how hard this attack is.

## Red flags
- Speaking only a few words per breath
- SpO2 below 92% on room air
- Rescue inhaler not working
